# CFD Reproduction of Forced Convection in Helical Microchannels Using Nanofluids

## Project Overview

This project reproduces a published numerical study of forced convection heat transfer in straight and helical microchannels using two water-based nanofluids: Al₂O₃–water and TiO₂–water.

A two-phase mixture model was employed to investigate heat transfer and pressure drop, with the governing equations discretized using the finite volume method. The original study validated the numerical model against experimental data for straight and helical microchannels.

## Simulation Parameters

* **Working fluids:** Water-based Al₂O₃ and TiO₂ nanofluids
* **Geometries:** Helical microchannels
* **Numerical approach:** Computational fluid dynamics (CFD)
* **Multiphase model:** Mixture model
* **Geometric parameters:** Helical pitch and curvature ratio
* **Performance metrics:** Nusselt number, friction factor, and pressure drop

## Key Findings from the Original Study

* The heat transfer performance depended on microchannel geometry, nanoparticle type, and nanoparticle concentration.
* The reported Nusselt number for Al₂O₃–water nanofluid in a helical microchannel was approximately 14% higher than that of pure water under the investigated conditions.
* The addition of nanoparticles had a relatively small effect on the friction factor in the helical microchannel.
* A performance evaluation parameter was used to identify operating conditions that balance thermal enhancement and hydraulic losses.

## Project Objectives

* Reproduce the numerical methodology reported in the published study.
* Investigate the effects of nanofluid properties and microchannel geometry on heat transfer and pressure drop.
* Evaluate the influence of helical pitch and curvature ratio on thermal-hydraulic performance.
* Compare reproduced results with the findings reported in the original publication.
